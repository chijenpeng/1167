# UREI 1176LN (revision F) as a circuit model

`urei1176::Channel` (`Source/dsp/Urei1176.h`) is one channel of a UREI 1176LN limiting amplifier,
serial suffix F (2611 to 7052, the last blackface units with a transformer input), modelled
component for component from UREI's own drawing: the bridged-T Input attenuator and input
transformer, the FET gain cell, preamp, side chain, discrete line amp and output transformer,
with the hardware's controls (Input, Output, Attack with its OFF click, Release, the four ratio
buttons in any of the six states the plug-in offers, the meter switch). Component values with
reference designators: [schematic reference](../reference/schematic.md).

It extends analog-recreation's verified `Fet1176` (the same circuit from Q1 to C15, with the
controls of an Origin Cali76 pedal and an ideal input buffer). Everything from that model that
was verified there is kept; what changed is listed under "Differences from the reference model".

## What is modelled, block by block

gives one), **Derived** (computed from documented behaviour).

All semiconductors of the gain cell, preamp and side chain (Q1 and its two gate junctions,
Q2-Q4, Q12-Q15, CR3, CR4: 19 unknowns) are one Newton group in one network, as in the reference,
so the compression loop closes within the sample. The line amp (Q5-Q9, CR1, CR2: 12 unknowns) is
a second group in its own network, fed by an ideal copy of the preamp output (R27's 100 k load
stays in the first network). No other groups were added: the input pad, T1 and T2 are linear and
live in those two networks.

### The Input attenuator's law

The drawing gives the topology but no values for R1A / R1B; the part was a two-deck Allen-Bradley
control with custom tapers (today PEC makes the replacement). Two pieces of evidence exist:

- One pulled rev B unit (GroupDIY): R1A 300-330 k and R1B ≈ 35 k at the ends; at "mid-rotation"
  R1A ≈ 5.5-6.5 k and R1B ≈ 80 Ω, i.e. 19-21 dB of loss.
- The dial is printed ∞ 48 36 30 24 18 12 6 0 at 40° steps (UA's reissue art; the original UREI
  art was not readable in any scan found), which reads as dB of loss: 24 at 12 o'clock.

The model's law is the dial (loss in dB = the printed number, interpolated in angle between
labels), with the end values of the measured unit. Evidence for choosing the dial over the
mid-rotation measurement: with it, Input and Output at 24 give +0.30 dB, matching UREI's and
UA's "both at 24 for unity gain"; with the measured 20.6 dB at mid-rotation they would give
+3.7 dB. The mid-rotation measurement is a single unit read by eye on a steep part of the law
(6 dB per 40°). Approximation, flagged.

### T2's turns ratio (a deviation from the brief's "1:1")

The brief asked for a 1:1 600 Ω output transformer (163-12614 / LL5402 class). Rev F uses the
B11148 instead (UREI's notes: rev F/G B11148, 16-12614 from rev I), and the drawing wires both
its primaries and both its secondaries in parallel. No data on the B11148 itself was found, but
four things agree on a step-up of about √3:

1. Sowter's replacement for the B11148 (1486) is "50+50 / 150+150 Ω": 1:1.73 per winding pair,
   which is exactly how the drawing connects it.
2. UREI specifies +24 dBm into 600 Ω (12.3 V rms, 17.4 V peak). The line amp runs from +30 V
   and clips near ±13.5 V peak at C15: 1:1 cannot deliver it, 1:1.73 can (≈ +26 dBu unloaded).
3. With 1:1.73 the model's maximum gain is 39.1 dB. The later units (rev H on, electronic input)
   are specified 45 ±1 dB, and UREI's revision notes say rev G's "bridging input" made the
   threshold 6 dB lower than rev F's: rev F ≈ 39 dB. With 1:1 the model gives 34.4 dB.
4. With 1:1.73 and Input / Output at "24" the model passes +0.30 dB, UREI's documented unity
   setting; with 1:1, −4.4 dB.

So T2 is modelled 1:√3 into the 10 kΩ bridging load. Its remaining parameters are approximations:
primary inductance 2.5 H (so that C15's series resonance with it stays inside UREI's response
test: 20 Hz within +0.5 dB), winding resistances 4 Ω / 27 Ω (output impedance 39 Ω, which is what
UREI's test 7 implies: a 600 Ω load costs 0.55 dB), leakage ≈0.4 mH (LL5402-like), core loss
1.5 kΩ across the primary. T2's winding capacitance is left out on purpose: with ≈0.4 mH of
leakage it would resonate near 400 kHz with a Q of about 6 into a bridging load, which the
trapezoidal rule (no oversampling) folds down to about 23 kHz at 48 kHz; its true effect in the
audio band is below 0.05 dB.

Consequence worth knowing: C15 (470 µF) and T2's primary form a series resonance near 4.6 Hz.
With nothing across the secondary (rev F) and a bridging load it is lightly damped: the model's
response is +0.02 dB at 20 Hz, +0.5 dB at 10 Hz and about +10 dB around 5 Hz. Real units' core
inductance and losses at such low levels are unknown, so the size of that subsonic peak is an
approximation; its existence follows from the circuit.

### The Attack knob's OFF position (S9) and the Attack Off switch

Every drawing from the original 1176 on shows "S9 G.R. disable switch located on R77" (R55 on the
earliest). It is a changeover at the GR amp's input: in normal operation the ratio buttons' B bus
feeds node 22 (C17 to Q12's base); with the Attack knob clicked fully counter-clockwise node 22 is
grounded and the bus cut. The rectifier, attack / release network and Q1's bias are not switched,
so Q1 stays at its idle bias: "units that have been properly biased and have the limiting
switched off will have approximately 1 db less gain" (factory test 4). The model has exactly that
switch (two switch elements in the netlist), not a bypass. The plug-in's control for S9 is the Attack Off switch (parameter `attackOff`); the knob no longer enters the OFF stretch (the Attack parameter keeps it, so old automation works, and a value there counts as off); the circuit behaviour is unchanged. `setLimitOff(true)` and ratio None both
give a gain-cell reduction of −0.05 dB (the 4:1-20:1 idle point); All with limiting off gives
−1.0 dB (All lowers the bias, Q1 conducts less than at the calibrated idle).

### Ratio None and All

None: no B section closed (the GR amp has no input) and no A section closed (the rectifier
reference hangs on R46 10M from a1). The model stays finite and does not limit; UREI's
troubleshooting table lists "no compression ratio button is depressed" as a cause of "no
limiting". All: every switch closed, the A taps shorted together, so the chain draws more current
and the bias moves down (Q1 gate −2.44 V instead of −1.52 V); the B taps are paralleled. The
"UA4706 T+C *" factory-selected part drawn at S4B was not identified and is not modelled.

### The meter

The GR meter of the drawing is Q10 (selected with Q1) as a source follower on the control node
through R48 3.9M, then IC1 and CR11 into the VU movement. Its current is linear in the control
voltage; UREI zeroes it with R55 at idle with limiting off and sets R54 so that 10 dB of real gain
reduction reads −10 (test 11). The model computes the same linear law from the control node
(zeroed at the operating point with limiting off, slope from the gain cell's settled 10 dB point)
instead of simulating Q10 and IC1. Two consequences: the needle reads the control voltage, which
moves about twice as far as Q1's gate on fast changes (the gate gets half of a fast change until
C5 follows, about 1 s), so it over-reads transients the way the hardware's would; and with all
buttons in, the lower bias makes it read above 0 at idle (the model pins it at +3).

+4 / +8: the VU movement (3.9 kΩ with its rectifier) across T2's secondary through R51 3.6 kΩ
(0 VU = 1.228 V = +4 dBu, the standard) or R50 8.2 kΩ (0 VU = +8.16 dBu by the resistor ratio).
The meter's load on the secondary (7.5 kΩ, nonlinear) is not modelled: the meter switch does not
change the audio, as the brief asks (the real meter costs less than 0.05 dB through 39 Ω).

Ballistics: one second-order movement, ζ = 0.8127, ωn = 13.512 rad/s (1.25 % overshoot, 99 % in
300 ms), driven by the full-wave rectified voltage in +4 / +8 and by the GR law in GR mode, with a
stop at rest and one beyond +3. `getGainReductionDb()` is separate: the block peak of the gain
cell's own reduction (from Q1's gate with the audio removed, see `controlGateSourceVolts`), not
the meter's law, for a bar display.

## Levels and calibration

- `calibrationDbu` (10-26, default 18): 0 dBFS (a full-scale sine) stands for that many dBu at the
  input and at the output. Input: the 600 Ω source's EMF is 2 × 0.7746 V × √2 × 10^(cal/20) per
  1.0, i.e. the float is the level delivered into a matched 600 Ω load. The unit's actual input
  impedance (≈675 Ω through T1 and R5) makes the terminal level 0.9 dB higher than that.
- Output: the float is the voltage across the 10 kΩ load over the same volts per unit.
- With these definitions Input and Output at 24 give +0.30 dB below threshold, unchanged by the
  calibration (both ends scale together); the calibration sets how hard a given float level drives
  the circuit.

### Operating point

Against the only drawing with marked voltages (rev K, gyraf.dk), the netlist built with the rev K
values agrees within ±5 % (±0.15 V minimum) everywhere and within 0.5 V on the line amp, whose
marked voltages disagree with each other by 0.4 V. The rev F build shares the side chain's and the
gain cell's voltages within the same tolerance (scaled to its 30.0 V rail); its line amp sits at
15.5 V, near half its rail. Full table: [schematic reference](../reference/schematic.md#dc-operating-point).
Q1's gate idles at −1.52 V (R81's wiper 742 Ω from ground, 0.371 of its track).

A stereo instance runs faster than real time at 96 kHz, but not comfortably (≈1.2x, i.e. about
85 % of one core). The cost is the engine's: at 96 kHz both models take one Newton iteration per
sample and run within 4 % of each other, so the input pad, T1 and T2 add almost nothing (they are
linear and share the existing networks). At 48 kHz the class AB line amp needs up to 3 Newton
iterations per sample on loud passages (1.0 at 96 kHz; the same with rev K's line amp and with
T2 removed), which is where the 48 kHz difference to the reference comes from (different levels
in the two runs). The profile at 96 kHz: dense 19 x 19 and 12 x 12 Newton solves 42 %, Jacobian
assembly 21 %, port bookkeeping 12 %, the linear solves 16 %. The 1176's numerical equations and
Newton groups are unchanged; the optional `solveSmall` kernel above would give +17 %.

## The fast engine

The plug-in runs `urei1176::FastChannel` (`Source/dsp/Fast1176.{h,cpp}`, `FastNet.h`). It has
`Channel`'s API and zero latency; the circuit engine is kept as the reference for tests and fits.

### Method

Every linear block is built from the circuit engine's component values by the trapezoidal rule
(`TrapNet` turns a small netlist into a discrete state-space system A, B, C, D), so it has the
reference's bilinear response at every sample rate. Nonlinear currents are injected into the linear
plant and solved with the feedthrough eliminated (K method). Per sample there are two scalar
solves, Q1 and the rectifier, a 2 x 2 solve for the GR amp's Q12 and Q14, and a bracketed Newton /
bisection on the control voltage that closes the loop inside the sample (a one-sample delay was
tried and over-charges at onsets).

| Block | Provenance |
|---|---|
| Input: 600 ohm source, bridged-T, T1, R5 | Circuit (linear, state space, rebuilt when Input moves) |
| Gain cell: R6, Q1, gate network R7 / R9 / C3 / C4 / C5, C6 | Circuit; Q1 is the reference's softplus square law (shared table) |
| Preamp Q2-Q4 | Derived: small-signal circuit with gm values baked from the reference DC operating point |
| Ratio ladder, GR amp, loading (cross-impedance of C19 / C20) | Circuit (linear, one block per ratio) |
| GR amp Q12 / Q14 (input and second stage) | Circuit, large signal: Ebers-Moll junctions, the exponential excess beyond the small-signal model, 2 x 2 Newton warm-started from the last sample; the load currents C19 / C20 draw enter from the previous sample |
| GR amp Q13 / Q15 (emitter followers at 5-7 mA) | Derived: small-signal (solving all four changes no figure beyond the rounding of the first two) |
| CR3 / CR4 rectifier | Circuit: FD333 leaky-diode law, log-domain Lambert-W start plus Newton |
| Attack, release, threshold reference, bias | Circuit; bias currents baked from the reference operating point |
| Output pot, line amp, C15, T2, load | Derived: small-signal line amp; T2 linear, as in the reference |
| Meters | Same laws as the circuit engine |
| Preamp output limit, line amp rails, gate clamp, operating-point slide | Approximation, fitted to the reference (constants at the top of `Fast1176.cpp`; refitted, see "The overload fit") |

### What was dropped

The large-signal solution of the preamp and line amp transistors (their overload is a fitted soft
limit plus an operating-point shift), Q1's gate junction currents (only a clamp remains), the
exponential of the GR amp's emitter followers Q13 and Q15, and the full coupled Newton groups. The
GR amp's former fitted soft limits on its two outputs are gone: Q12 and Q14 are solved as
transistors. As in the reference there is no Early effect and no BJT capacitance.
Idle starts from the reference's DC-mode state (1e-10 S leaks leave the control node about 0.5 mV
off the bias).

### The All-mode envelope error is not the overload

The largest remaining program error is the vocals / bass All envelope (p99 0.81 / 0.68 dB). Measured with
a diagnostic build of the fast engine (switches that bypass each fitted limit, counters of how often each is
active):

- At ~12 dB GR on the program files none of the fitted limits is active: the preamp output limit and
  its slide, the line amp's rails and slide, and the gate clamp each ran on 0.000 of the samples, and bypassing
  any or all of them gives bit-identical output. The overload constants cannot move this error.
- The error sits in the quiet windows. In the 50 ms windows above -50 dBFS (the audible bulk of the vocals) the mean envelope error
  is 0.006 dB and the worst 0.24 dB (0.013 and 0.60 dB for vocals 20:1). In the windows between -60 and -70 dBFS (the gaps between phrases) it is
  0.5 dB on average and 0.85 at worst for vocals All, 1.0 dB on average and 2.1 at worst for vocals 20:1.
- In those windows the output is dominated by content below 12 Hz (about 20 dB above everything else in the window): the slow tail that the
  preceding loud passage leaves in the coupling and the output transformer. The fast engine reproduces it to 20 % at 20:1,
  8 % in All, 2 % with the ratio None (the average spectrum over the loud windows agrees within 0.01 dB in every octave band). It is
  larger with gain reduction, so it comes from the control voltage's thump through the cell and the preamp's operating-point
  slide with signal, which the linearised preamp (Q2-Q4 at their idle bias) does not have. The windows are 60 to 70 dB under full scale
  and the content is subsonic.
- All-mode bias and threshold-chain values are right for All: static GR, thresholds and the GR trajectories in All match to 0.01 to 0.25 dB.

Not fixed (no cheap fix: it needs the preamp's large-signal DC shift, a Q2-Q4 solve).

## Engine changes

`Source/dsp/circuit` is a local adaptation of the circuit engine used by this model. Unused triode,
pentode, op-amp, and CMOS-inverter models and devices were removed, along with their dedicated
solvers and affine-device shortcut. This plug-in uses the generic device-group solver with JFETs,
BJTs, and diodes. The equations and generic Newton path used by those devices did not change:

- `DeviceGroup` dispatches directly to its general Newton solver; the 1176 never instantiated any
  of the removed devices, so its audio results are unchanged.

One real-time fix was also needed:

- `LinearNetwork::rebuildPortResponses()` allocated a temporary vector on every refactor, and a
  refactor runs inside `process()` whenever a pot or switch moves. It now uses a member vector
  sized in `prepare()`, and `prepare()` reserves worst-case capacity for the sparse LU and the
  coupling lists `factorise()` refills. Results are bit-identical. `plugin1167_test rt` counts
  allocations while every control moves: zero.

Not applied (measured, available): replacing `solveSmall` (the dense Newton solve) for 5 ≤ n ≤ 24
by a version with rows padded to a fixed stride (same pivoting, results equal to rounding) makes
this model 17 % faster (96 kHz: 2.44x to 2.84x real time per channel). Sparse P / C products in
`DeviceGroup::solve` added only 3 %.

- **Supplies** are ideal (+30.0 V, −10 V): no regulator dynamics, ripple or sag.

## Stereo link (the 1176SA tie)

The rev F drawing's "stereo interconnect" J1 lands on the control node (C27, R7, R48). UREI's
1176SA adapter ties two units' control nodes through a floating voltage (a battery and a pot, to
null the two units' different idle control voltages), so both gain cells follow the larger of the
two side chains; the attack / release networks are then in parallel (forum reports: the fastest
attack about doubles). The adapter's own values were not found (reports disagree: a 1.4 V cell or a
9 V battery; no pot value). **Provenance: Derived.** The plug-in models the tie of the control nodes
with the offset source nulled (0 V: two identical units have no idle difference); the adapter's
values stay unverified, which is why there is no offset.

What the fast engine solves (`FastChannel::processLinked`; `Impl::solveRectifierPair`,
`stepFinishLink`). Each unit keeps its own cell, GR amp, CR3 / CR4 and attack path (R + C22). The
shared node is `x = kapS (I_a + I_b + c0_a + c0_b)` with `kapS = 1 / (g27_a + g27_b + 1/R_rel_a +
1/R_rel_b)`: both rectifier currents in, both C27s' companion sources, both bias returns and
gate-network draws. Unit k's cathodes sit at `x + (R_attack + rho) I_k + vz0_k`, so the two
rectifier currents are coupled through the node: a 2 x 2 Newton solve with `I_k = i3 + i4` of
the two diode pairs, started per unit from the single-unit Lambert-W solution of the pair's mean
and accepted on the single-unit criterion, then the loop of `loopStep` (bracketed Newton, the
same safeguards) on the one shared `x`, with the slope of the pair
(`M dI = q`, `M = [[1 + J_a (r_a + kapS), J_a kapS], [J_b kapS, 1 + J_b (r_b + kapS)]]`). With
two identical units the iteration is the single unit's, so Link equals Dual (see the tests below).
Zero latency, no allocation, the same stages as `processPair`.

Engaging the tie while playing: the two units' control voltages differ, so the shared node starts at
their mean and each cell keeps seeing the control voltage it had (an offset per unit, an addition
to the cell's gate input only) which decays with a 15 ms time constant; untying folds the
offsets back, so nothing steps. This glide is a choice for the plug-in, not circuit behaviour.

Measured (`plugin1167_stereo_test`, 48 kHz, 4:1, Input full, 1 kHz tone about 14 dB over the
threshold):

## The S/C HPF (a plug-in addition)

A first-order high-pass on the detector, choices OFF / 30 / 60 / 105 / 125 / 185 Hz. **Provenance: Custom**; the 1176 has no such filter, and the reference
engine (`urei1176::Channel`) ignores the control, so the development comparison against it is an OFF comparison.

Where it sits. The 1176 is a feedback compressor: the GR amp's input is the preamp output `preIn`, which
the control voltage itself decides, so `preIn` is solved inside every sample (bracketed Newton in
`stepFinish`, the joint solve in `stepFinishLink`). The filter sits on `preIn` in front of the GR amp, inside
that solve: `hp = preIn - g lp` with `lp` the one-pole low-pass of `preIn` (`k = 1 - exp(-2 pi fc / fs)`, `lp' = lp + k (preIn - lp)`),
that is `hp = hpF preIn + hpC` with the feedthrough `hpF = 1 - g k` and the history term `hpC = -g (1 - k) lp`,
both known before the sample is solved. `sideAnodes` feeds the GR amp `hp`; `loopSlopes` and `channelSlopes` (so the
Link path) multiply the preamp slope `dpre` by `hpF`, which keeps the Newton step exact; `sideCommit` advances the GR amp with `hp`, advances `lp`
with the solved `preIn` and prepares the next sample's `hpF` and `hpC`. The attack-off (`active`) test is unchanged.
`g` (0 off, 1 on) and `k` glide linearly over 10 ms when the choice changes (the corner is taken at once while off); `lp` keeps running
while off so that enabling does not meet a stale state. With `g = 0`, `hpF` is exactly 1 and `hpC` exactly -0: OFF is bit-identical to the
engine before the filter existed (checked on vocals, drums, a bass DI and a program mix in Dual, Link and M/S). The corner comes from the sample rate
`prepare()` was given (44.1 to 192 kHz). In M/S the units see M and S, so the filter acts on those detectors.
